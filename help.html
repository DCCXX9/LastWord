Your library, your display names

Get started

  1. In Settings, enter your Last.fm username and select Connect profile. You never enter your Last.fm password into the extension.
  2. Open your own Last.fm Library and refresh the page. Features apply only to the connected profile.
  3. Open Import iTunes XML to scan an exported library locally. Review the artist, album and song names, tick the preferences you want, then Apply.
  4. Use Display names to search, edit or delete your preferences. Keep a JSON backup from Settings.

Names imported from XML match existing spellings ignoring case. They do not merge different spellings or rename Last.fm catalogue entries.

What the beta does

  • Display names: your chosen casing on your own profile, including library headings. XML import and display preferences do not edit your Last.fm scrobbles.
  • Keep scrolling: asks before loading more pages. Yes loads once; No stops this view; Always loads as you scroll until the next ten-page checkpoint (about 500 rows). The first page counts toward the limit. New views ask again. Scrobble date breaks and chronological order are preserved.
  • Personal covers: on a specific album’s library page, every track uses that album’s header cover and album link. Other track lists use the album you have scrobbled most for a track. Ties use the most recent album. Blank album tags use the artist’s album page with the track’s title. Both the image and its album link change.
  • Broken images: failed artist portraits and album thumbnails retry alternate sizes of the same selected image, including rows added while scrolling. Links and chosen artwork stay intact. Deleted images or images blocked at every size cannot be recovered.
  • Track editing: Edit track in album menus opens Last.fm’s editor on the current page. Real edits still use Last.fm’s forms and your existing editing access. Casing-only edits can be saved locally.

Artist redirect protection

Enabled by default after connecting your profile. Travis Scott and Lucki links use Last.fm’s +noredirect route in your profile and on their public music pages, including album and track links and rows loaded while scrolling. Turn off Protect artist redirects in Settings to restore native links.

Genuine Lucky Twice entries remain unchanged. A link already redirected to Lucky Twice or Travi$ Scott is only repaired when that row’s original native scrobble fields verify Lucki or Travis Scott. Display-name preferences alone never determine artist identity.

If your charts still combine artists, review automatic spelling corrections in Last.fm website settings. This extension does not change that account setting. Last.fm explains the limitation.

Protection applies to links on these pages and the extension’s library/artwork reads. An unprotected URL typed into the address bar or opened from another website may redirect before the extension runs. Global statistics, merged catalogue data and mobile apps remain controlled by Last.fm. Image-gallery URLs and native edit forms stay native.

Cover loading

Unverified covers and album links stay hidden while your preferred album is found. Fresh verified covers are reused immediately. Scroll-added rows receive cached and shared covers before their text appears; new lookups get up to four seconds of preparation, then continue near the screen.

Public artist pages and their Tracks lists also use your most-scrobbled album. If Last.fm confirms you have never heard a track, its exact source-album association may supply a cover. Similar tracks and unrelated albums are never substituted.

Beta limitations

New, uncached covers require paced requests to Last.fm and can take time, especially for loose tracks on long lists. Instant covers for every unseen track are not guaranteed. A genuinely missing cover or exact album association cannot be supplied; the image slot may be empty.

Artwork and native dialogs depend on Last.fm’s current page structure. If a form or album history cannot be verified, the extension reports a limitation instead of guessing.

Automatic album-less corrections are unavailable in this beta. Saved rules and discovery records remain available for review, but this version does not automatically clear album fields on future scrobbles. Any previous enable setting is retained without being acted on. Read-only discovery is optional and runs only when you explicitly start a batch.

Preferences stay in this browser installation. They do not sync to other devices, other users or Last.fm’s servers. Some native editing features depend on your Last.fm account access.

Back up, update and undo

Before updating: export JSON in Settings & backup. Keep your existing unpacked extension folder in place, click Reload in chrome://extensions, then refresh Last.fm. Do not remove the extension to update it.

Undo XML: Revert last import restores the preferences changed by the latest import and preserves later manual edits. Applying another import replaces that undo record. A JSON export gives you a longer-lasting backup.

Restore JSON: review the preview before confirming. Restore replaces display preferences and profile settings, and clears the latest XML undo action. Existing newer discovery records are preserved.

Changing installations: a future store installation may have a different extension identity and storage. Export first; do not uninstall your working copy before checking the new one.

If something stops working

  • Check the connected username in the extension popup. After Reload, refresh the Last.fm tab once.
  • If Last.fm asks you to take a break, leave requests paused. Do not repeatedly restart discovery.
  • For feedback, use the support channel on the beta download or store listing. The popup’s Copy diagnostics button supplies version, configured username, feature status and counts. Review it before sharing; it does not include song titles, XML contents or security tokens.
  • To pause the extension completely, disable it in chrome://extensions. This retains its local data. Uninstalling can remove that data, so export first.
